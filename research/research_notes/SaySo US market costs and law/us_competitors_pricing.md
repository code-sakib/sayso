# US Competitors and Pricing: In-Person Sales Conversation Intelligence and Retail Video Analytics

Research date: 2026-10-28. Almost no vendor in either category publishes prices. Most price figures below come from third parties (Sacra, competitor "pricing guides", resellers, marketplaces) and are labelled **[3P est.]**. Competitor-written guides (SalesAsk, Craftflow, Roonly, Surveillant, Spot AI blog) have an incentive to frame rivals as expensive, so read them that way.

## 1. In-person conversation intelligence: who competes, pricing, funding, ARR

### Takeaway
The US in-person category is dominated by two well-funded home-services players, Rilla (~$51M ARR in 2025, ~$757M valuation) and Siro ($50M Series B). Both price at roughly $200-$420 per rep per month on annual contracts with seat minimums. Retail-floor-specific tools are small or early. The closest direct analog to SaySo is **Ethosphere** (Seattle, $2.5M pre-seed, Sept 2025), which records only the associate's side of in-store conversations with wearable mics, the same approach as SaySo. None of these tools combine audio with CCTV.

### Cited Findings

**Rilla (NYC, founded 2019): the category leader, starting to move into retail**
- Sacra estimates ARR at $11M (2023), $24M (2024), $51M (2025) and $70M (April 2026), with 112.5% YoY growth in 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Series B in June 2025: $68.5M primary at ~$757M post-money, led by GV, with Bessemer, HubSpot Ventures and others. Series A (Dec 2022) was $3.7M at ~$45M post. Total raised is ~$72-79M — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/); [Forge](https://forgeglobal.com/rilla_ipo/)
- More than 2,000 customer accounts by mid-2025. Implied ACV rose from ~$9.3K (early 2024) to ~$20K (mid-2025). The Home Depot was named as a customer in January 2026. The original ICP was home-services contractors with $5M-$50M revenue and 5-50 field reps, expanding into home building, dental, retail and enterprise — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Pricing [3P est.]: **$4,000-$5,000 per seat per year (~$333-$417/rep/mo), 5-seat minimum (~$20K floor)**, annual contracts paid upfront, 3% annual escalator, with premium add-ons (Rilla Live, Rilla Intelligence). Gross margin is ~85-90% and AI/transcription cost is under $1 per appointment — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Other estimates [3P est., competitor-authored]: **$199-$349/rep/mo** on annual contracts, 5-seat minimum, **one-time setup fee of $1,500-$5,000** — [SalesAsk guide](https://www.salesask.com/alternatives/rilla/rilla-pricing-guide-2026). Craftflow puts Rilla at $2,400-$3,600/rep/yr — [Craftflow](https://www.craftflow.com/compare/rilla). These conflict with Sacra's higher figure.
- An older Latka estimate ($14M ARR in 2024, $42M valuation) conflicts with Sacra and looks out of date — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/rilla.com)
- Uses a microphone worn by the associate; the Borentis category map lists Rilla's targets as physical retail and in-home sales — [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)

**Siro (in-person sales coaching via phone app)**
- $50M Series B led by SignalFire, announced May 22, 2025, with 01 Advisors and StepStone — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/siro-raises-50m-series-b-to-bring-ai-to-in-person-sales-302463711.html)
- Total raised is $68M according to [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/siro) and ~$75M according to [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/). Sacra estimates Rilla's revenue at roughly 3-4x Siro's — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Pitches to teams "where customers come to you: your store, showroom, dealership, clinic" — [Siro](https://www.siro.ai/)
- Pricing [3P est., estimates vary widely]: ~$250/rep/mo annual plus implementation fees — [Roonly](https://roonly.ai/compare/siro); $200-$350/rep/mo — [Fervor Studio](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/siro-review-pricing-alternatives/); $3,000+/user/yr — [Craftflow](https://www.craftflow.com/transparency/worth-it/siro)
- Has a deep integration with ServiceTitan — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)

**Ethosphere (Seattle): the closest direct analog to SaySo**
- Raised $2.5M pre-seed in September 2025, led by Point72 Ventures, with AI2 Incubator, Pack VC and others — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/16/ethosphere-raises-2-5m-support-retail-associates-ai-insights/); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/?p=890213)
- Uses wearable microphones and LLMs on **only the employee's side** of in-store conversations. Associates get coaching and managers get insights. Retailers handle in-store disclosures. Customers include QSR chains, clothing shops and luxury retailers; it has some revenue plus custom pilots with large chains — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/16/ethosphere-raises-2-5m-support-retail-associates-ai-insights/)
- Comments on a RetailWire discussion criticised grading a conversation while ignoring the customer's side — [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/recording-in-store-associates/)
- Pricing not found.

**Other retail-floor in-person tools (mostly small, unpriced)**
- **Vera AI (heyvera.ai):** dedicated associate device (it also runs on a PC or phone) that scores conversations against 50+ criteria. It shows logos including Boggi, Salomon, Nissan, Hyundai and GAC, and has an English/Spanish site. It claims mystery shopping covers "at best, 0.2%" of interactions. HQ location, pricing and funding are not stated, and it does not appear to be US-centric — [Vera AI](https://www.heyvera.ai/)
- **FrontlineIQ:** in-person coaching for furniture showroom and appliance associates — [FrontlineIQ](https://www.frontlineiq.ai/)
- **InStore.ai:** US, convenience and frontline retail, capture method not disclosed — [InStore.ai](https://www.instore.ai/product); [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)
- **Revenue.io in-person module:** captures via a phone app, aimed at showrooms and field reps — [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)
- **Verint and NICE:** fixed counter microphones in bank branches, telecom and luxury showrooms — [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)
- Non-US players include tolqAI (Gulf), Cognifyze (EU, fixed sensor), and Borentis, Supereps and Darwix (India) — [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)
- **Craftflow (Craft):** home-improvement, positioned as "we'll beat your Rilla quote" [3P est.]: $80-$200/user/mo — [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/sales-tools/craftflow)

**Adjacent tools and price anchors**
- **Gong** [3P est.]: ~$1,300-$1,600/user/yr plus a platform fee of $5K-$50K/yr. One source says the platform fee moved to $50K under a March 2025 pricing model. Add-ons (Forecast, Engage) cost ~$700-$800/user/yr, plus onboarding of several thousand dollars — [Revenue.io](https://www.revenue.io/blog/what-does-gong-actually-cost); [MarketBetter](https://marketbetter.ai/blog/gong-pricing-breakdown-2026/). Gong's valuation is $7.25B; ZoomInfo bought Chorus for $575M in 2021 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- **Hyperbound** (AI roleplay, not recording): $15M Series A in Sept 2025 led by Peak XV, ~$18.3M total — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/hyperbound-raises-usd-15-mn-to-expand-ai-sales-training/497218)
- **Salesken** (India/US, call-centric): $22M Series B in 2021 led by M12/Sequoia; no newer round found — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/salesken-raises-22-mn-in-series-b-funding-from-microsofts-m12)
- **Spiro** (AI CRM, not in-person CI): $79-$95/user/mo annual on its pricing page — [Spiro](https://spiro.ai/pricing)
- **Car dealerships: Toma** (AI phone voice agents, not floor coaching): $17M Series A led by a16z in June 2025, in 100+ dealerships. One reported example has a dealer group paying ~$3,500/mo [3P] — [Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/technology/toma-secures-17-million-in-funding-to-build-ai-agents-for-dealerships/); [Macha guide](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/toma-ai-complete-guide)
- **Podium** (messaging/reviews for local businesses and dealers) [3P]: Core $399/mo, Pro $599/mo, annual contracts, add-ons such as a $99/mo AI module — [SkillMammoth](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/podium-pricing); [Astucia](https://astucia.io/blog/podium-pricing-2026-what-smbs-actually-pay)
- **Wearable hardware sold for dealerships:** Dezhu/ZKJ sells a clip-on "AI recording badge" for car-dealership and retail reception script compliance (vendor of Chinese origin) — [ZKJ](https://www.zkj.com/en/ai_recording_badge)

### Inferences
- The US price anchor for in-person coaching is about **$200-$420 per rep per month, annual upfront, 5-seat minimum, with a setup fee**. Price at that level would read as premium for retail floor staff, whose wages are far below those of home-services closers. That leaves room for SaySo to price per store or per device rather than per rep.
- Rilla's move into retail (The Home Depot, a "physical retail" segment) makes it the most likely well-funded entrant into SaySo's space. Ethosphere is the closest product-level twin, though tiny.
- No product found in this set adds CCTV or video to the audio layer.

### Gaps
- Could not identify "Ambient/Tough Day" or "Rainbird" as in-person sales CI vendors. Ambient.ai is a security video company. No sources were found for these names in this context.
- No disclosed prices for Ethosphere, Vera AI, FrontlineIQ or InStore.ai. No US mattress- or jewelry-specific recording vendor was found. Dealer.ai was not researched.
- ARR for Siro is only inferred from Sacra's "Rilla is 3-4x" (which implies roughly $13-17M in 2025; this is inference, not reported).

## 2. Wearable recorders used by businesses

### Takeaway
Consumer AI recorders are cheap ($50-$180 hardware, roughly $0-$20/mo software), and Big Tech has absorbed the leaders: Amazon bought Bee and Meta bought Limitless. No enterprise retail-floor deployment of these consumer devices was found. B2B in-person CI vendors mostly use phones (Siro, Revenue.io) or their own mics (Rilla, Ethosphere, Vera).

### Cited Findings
- **Plaud NotePin:** ~$159-$179 hardware. Free tier of 300 min; Pro ~$8.33/mo annual; Unlimited ~$20/mo (other sources say $99.99/yr Pro, $239.99/yr Unlimited) — [Pastok](https://pastok.com/blog/best-ai-wearables); [Intriq](https://www.intriq.app/blog/best-ai-wearables/)
- **Limitless pendant:** Meta acquired it on Dec 5, 2025 and stopped selling the pendant; the team joined Reality Labs — [Usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/limitless-ai-alternatives/); [Fieldy](https://www.fieldy.ai/blog/limitless-pendant-alternatives)
- **Bee:** Amazon acquisition confirmed July 2025; $49.99 hardware. Reports conflict on whether the $19/mo subscription remains — [Layer3Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/gear/reviews/bee-ai-pendant). An Amazon launch on Oct 15 (2026) of $75/$100 devices drew coverage framing it as a surveillance product — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/bee-ai-wearables-privacy-debate-2026/)
- **Omi** markets its wearable for sales teams — [Omi](https://www.omi.me/pages/sales)
- **Seeed reSpeaker Clip** is an open wearable recorder for building voice AI apps (a possible OEM option) — [Seeed](https://www.seeedstudio.com/blog/2026/07/13/respeaker-clip-an-open-wearable-ai-recorder-for-building-voice-ai-applications/)

### Inferences
- Hardware BOM and retail price points for a badge are commoditised. SaySo's defensibility must come from software and analytics plus video, not the device.

### Gaps
- No public enterprise retail deployment of Plaud, Bee or Limitless was found. Humane (AI Pin) was not researched; it is widely reported to have been shut down after HP bought its assets in 2025, which was not re-verified here.

## 3. Retail video / CCTV analytics in the US: players and pricing

### Takeaway
Video analytics pricing clusters at **~$146-$700 per store per month** for retail traffic and loss-prevention SaaS, or **~$5-$30 per camera per month** (up to ~$99 bundled with hardware) for cloud VMS with AI. Almost every vendor is quote-only. Big players include Sensormatic ShopperTrak, RetailNext, Solink, Verkada, Eagle Eye and Spot AI. Placer.ai (phone-location foot traffic, $100M ARR, $1.5B valuation) is adjacent but not camera-based.

### Cited Findings
- **Solink** (video plus POS for loss prevention and ops): starts at ~$175/mo per location on marketplace listings, priced per location rather than per camera — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/operations-management-software/a/solink/pricing/); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/solink/pricing). Another guide's estimate is $400-$700/store/mo [3P] — [Fora Soft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/video-surveillance-retail)
- **RetailNext** (traffic and in-store analytics): calculator estimate of ~$146/store/mo with hardware bundled [3P] — [StoreTraffic](https://storetraffic.com/best-people-counters/)
- **Sensormatic ShopperTrak:** quote-only, hardware price not published — [StoreTraffic](https://storetraffic.com/best-people-counters/)
- **Verkada:** publishes camera MSRPs only. License is ~$199/camera/yr on 1-year terms, ~$180/yr on longer terms; another benchmark says $199-$1,799/camera/yr. List prices were raised June 5, 2026 [3P] — [Surveillant](https://surveillant.ai/guides/verkada-pricing)
- **Eagle Eye Networks** (cloud VMS): ~$5-$30/camera/mo depending on resolution and retention; another guide says from $1,000/camera/yr (conflicting) [3P] — [Surveillant](https://surveillant.ai/guides/eagle-eye-networks-pricing)
- **Spot AI** (AI on existing CCTV; $40M raised in 2022, $62M total, "thousands" of US locations): ~$99/camera/mo bundled with appliance, vs ~$100-$200/camera/yr plus one-time recorder cost in another guide (sources differ ~6x) [3P] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/17/spot-ai-raises-40m-to-build-smarter-cctv-security-camera-tech/); [Surveillant](https://surveillant.ai/guides/spot-ai-pricing); [GetSafeAndSound](https://getsafeandsound.com/blog/spot-ai-pricing/)
- **Deep North** (CCTV to retail analytics: entries, occupancy, queue times, conversion): $25.7M Series A in 2020 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/12/deep-north-raises-25-7m-for-ai-that-uses-cctv-to-build-retail-analytics/)
- **Standard AI:** pivoted from autonomous checkout to AI cameras and launched "Vision Analytics" (dated Aug 2026). Last reported valuation was $1B; total raised is $236M-$289M depending on source — [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/standard-ai); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/185113-45)
- **Placer.ai** (mobile-location foot traffic, not CCTV): $75M at $1.5B valuation, $100M ARR — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h100twskqr)
- **Queue / wait-time camera tools:** Horus and FlowLinks offer queue monitoring on existing CCTV (no funding found). A vendor claims 20% of customers may leave after waiting over 3 minutes (unverified) — [Horus](https://horusapp.io/blog/ai-queue-management-for-retail/); [FlowLinks](https://www.flowlinks.ai/blog/retail-checkout-queue-management)

### Inferences
- A SaySo video add-on at roughly $100-$300 per store per month would sit inside the RetailNext/Solink band. Per-camera cloud VMS ($5-$30/cam/mo) sets the floor for "AI on my existing cameras".
- Spot AI, Solink and Verkada already sell "AI on existing cameras" to US multi-site retailers. Any walkout or long-wait detection SaySo offers would likely be matched as a feature by them. The differentiator is linking it to who was speaking to the customer, which comes from the audio.

### Gaps
- No verified pricing or recent funding found for Density, Pathr, VergeSense (office-focused), Everseen, or Hanwha/Axis analytics licenses. No dedicated "walkout detection" vendor with a 2025 US round was found.
- Sensormatic ShopperTrak per-store pricing is not public.

## 4. Has anyone combined in-store audio coaching with video walkout detection?

### Takeaway
No commercial product or patent was found that fuses associate audio coaching with CCTV walkout or wait detection. Audio-based associate monitoring (Walmart patent, Ethosphere, Rilla) and video-based associate-customer interaction detection (patents, Teradata, Deep North) exist separately.

### Cited Findings
- Walmart was granted a patent for "listening to the front end": audio sensors at checkout that analyze associate-customer conversations to create employee performance metrics (audio only) — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/walmart-wins-patent-for-in-store-audio-monitoring/527822/)
- US9569786 tracks salespeople and customers via video and flags "interactions" based on proximity (video only) — [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9569786)
- US8560357B2 covers retail video analytics; its cameras "may include audio capture", but the analytics focus on customer movement — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8560357B2/en)
- The 2026 in-store CI vendor map found no vendor combining audio with CCTV. Borentis mentions "video-ready" capture only — [Borentis map](https://borentis.com/learn/in-store-conversation-intelligence-vendors-map/)

### Inferences
- The fusion appears open. However, the Walmart audio patent and video-interaction patents are adjacent prior art, so a patent/FTO search is advisable before claiming novelty.

### Gaps
- No full-text patent claims search (USPTO/Google Patents) was done for combined microphone and camera associate scoring.

## 5. Customer evidence / ROI claims, and backlash

### Takeaway
ROI claims are vendor case studies (home services), typically **+7% to +80% close-rate lifts**, with no audited data. Backlash in retail centres on employee monitoring: Walmart's bodycam pilot, Walmart's audio patent, and Amazon Bee coverage. No public failure of an in-store audio coaching vendor was found.

### Cited Findings
- Rilla case studies report a 7% close-rate increase at Lifestyle Home Products, a rise from 23% to 42% at Total Comfort, and a +167% increase at Climate Experts — [Rilla – Lifestyle](https://legacy.rilla.com/testimonials/lifestyle-home-products); [Rilla – Total Comfort](https://legacy.rilla.com/testimonials/total-comfort-rilla); [Rilla – Climate Experts](https://www.rilla.com/customer-stories/climate-expert)
- A Rilla dental case reports case acceptance going from 10% to 40% (vendor-sourced) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Siro claims a 21% close-rate increase and 17% revenue growth in case studies, and one rep going from 48% to 68% — [Siro testimonials](https://www.siro.ai/testimonials)
- Craftflow markets "close 31% more deals" — [Craftflow](https://www.craftflow.com/in-home-sales)
- **Backlash: Walmart bodycams.** Walmart piloted bodycams for associates in the Dallas market (Dec 2024). United for Respect called it inadequate, and workers on Reddit feared it would be used for performance monitoring. Dollar General, Kroger and TJX are also testing them — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/walmart-employees-are-now-wearing-body-cameras-stores-rcna184516); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/12/17/walmart-experimenting-with-body-cameras-for-employees-shopper-violence); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/walmart-tests-in-store-body-cameras-for-employees/735868)
- The Walmart audio-monitoring patent drew privacy concern coverage — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/walmart-wins-patent-for-in-store-audio-monitoring/527822/)
- Experts warn that AI wearables that transcribe speech can violate state wiretap laws (commentary, not rulings) — [Corner of Fifth](https://corneroffifth.studio/ai-wearables-wiretapping-laws/). Coverage split on whether Amazon Bee is a surveillance product — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/bee-ai-wearables-privacy-debate-2026/)

### Inferences
- The US positioning should lead with "coaching, not surveillance" and an employee-only-audio design (as Ethosphere does), plus clear customer disclosure, to reduce wiretap and backlash risk.

### Gaps
- No retail-specific results data (conversion lift in stores) from Ethosphere, Vera or others. No lawsuit against an in-person CI vendor was found.

## 6. Typical US sales cycle and buyer

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Rilla sells annual, upfront, 5+ seat contracts with ~$20K ACV, mainly to owners and sales leaders of SMB contractors. A vendor source cites retail tech cycles of 45-150 days with Head of Store Operations as the buyer, and Q4 freezes followed by January evaluation windows.

### Cited Findings
- Rilla sells annual contracts paid upfront with a 3% escalator and 5-seat minimum; implied ACV is ~$20K; the ICP is 5-50 rep contractors — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rilla/)
- Retail tech: 45-150 day sales cycle, $20K-$250K ACV, Head of Store Operations as a key buyer; Q4 freezes and January evaluation windows [vendor marketing, low confidence] — [DevCommX](https://www.devcommx.com/sales-automation/scottsdale/retail-tech)
- Ethosphere sells via custom pilots with large chains before rollout — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/16/ethosphere-raises-2-5m-support-retail-associates-ai-insights/)
- Podium and most local-business SaaS require annual contracts — [Astucia](https://astucia.io/blog/podium-pricing-2026-what-smbs-actually-pay)

### Inferences
- Likely buyers are: the owner or dealer principal for single and small multi-store businesses (fast, weeks); VP Retail/Store Ops plus Loss Prevention (for video) at chains, with paid pilots and 3-6+ month cycles. This is inference, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No independent survey of who signs for in-store coaching tools at US chains or dealer groups. No Dealer.ai or dealership showroom-floor coaching pricing was found.
