5 Cost levers

Five levers, ranked by how much they save

Speech-to-text dominates, so the top levers cut either the hours transcribed or the price per hour.

#LeverSaves / store / monthCatch
1Cut silence on the badge (VAD): transcribe 78 of 182 h~₹8,400Must catch quiet speech in a noisy store
2Self-host speech-to-text (Omnilingual / Whisper on Modal L4, $0.80/h)~₹5,000–5,800Speaker separation + Hinglish accuracy still untested

4Cheaper analysis model~₹1,000Check coaching quality
5Video on the edge box; SAM 3.1 only on walk-out clipsavoids ~₹44,500Needs compatible store CCTV recorders

Avoid Meta's "contributor" pricing. It is about 10× cheaper but lets Meta train on the data, which clashes with DPDP rules and retailer contracts. On hardware, use a 1,000 mAh battery (500 mAh won't last a shift), record to SD and upload in batches.

7 The buyer's view

Sell extra sales, not saved hours

₹19,000 is 25–32% of a 5-person store's frontline salary bill (associates earn ₹10–15K a month), so "saves time" won't sell. "Wins back walkouts" will.

SaySo bill₹19,000per month, 5 badges
Store margin per phone≈ ₹2,40010% of ₹24,000 avg phone (assumption)
Break-even≈ 8 phonesextra sales a month, about 2 a week
= one month of SaySo

Jewellery clears the bar faster: one ring can pay for a month. Staff turnover in consumer durables is 66–71% a year, so classroom training keeps walking out the door. Always-on coaching stays.

8 Law (DPDP Act)

Recording customers needs their consent

The retailer is the "Data Fiduciary" (responsible). SaySo is its "Data Processor". Penalties go up to ₹250 crore for weak security.

BreachTell the retailer within hours (they have 72 h to report)

Open questions: whether a store sign counts as valid consent is untested in Indian courts, and voiceprints may need written consent under the old rules. Get an opinion from an Indian privacy lawyer before the first paid pilot.
